What’s New on the Table

Boca Raton and Delray Beach Restaurants, Fall 2026

Season has arrived in South Palm Beach County, and so has a new class of restaurants.
By Samantha Gornstein · The Edmund Bogen Team at Douglas Elliman · November 3, 2026

As someone who works and sells real estate across Boca Raton and Delray Beach every day, I watch where new restaurants choose to open. This year’s list tells a clear story: operators from New York, Connecticut and Miami want to be here.

Here’s how it breaks down. Boca’s newest arrivals lean toward cafés and neighborhood dining, while Delray has the season’s newest fine-dining room in Ethereal. Boca’s most anticipated arrival, Limani Grille, is now open at Town Center at Boca Raton. Two other big names, Felice and Fletchers, were still listed as coming, or had no confirmed opening date, when I went to press.

Mark Your CalendarThe Savor Boca Experience runs Saturday, November 14, from 1:30 to 4:30 p.m. across Mizner Park and downtown Boca. Sixteen restaurants and shops take part. Tickets are $65, or two for $100, and proceeds benefit the Rotary Club of Boca Raton.

Now Open in Boca Raton

Boca’s 2026 openings run from all-day cafés in West Boca to a 250-seat destination dining room.

Maman, West Boca

9959 Glades Road, Shadowood Square · mamannyc.com

The French café and bakery held its Boca Raton grand opening on October 6. Maman began in SoHo in 2014 and now has more than 60 locations across the U.S. and Canada. Expect pastries, specialty coffee, and scratch-made breakfast and lunch in a room dressed with vintage French furnishings.

Crema Gourmet, West Boca

3011 W. Yamato Road, Suite A-20 · cremagourmet.com

Crema Gourmet celebrated its West Boca opening with a long weekend starting October 22. The all-day breakfast café takes its cue from European cafés, with ahi tuna toast, Greek omelettes, healthy bowls, and sandwiches on multigrain baguettes. Downtown diners can also find Crema at 305 Plaza Real.

Boca Brasserie

5377 Town Center Road, Suite 200, Restaurant Row · bocabrasserie.com

Chef Pushkar Marathe and partner Andy Dugard turned Stage Kitchen & Bar into Boca Brasserie this summer, reopening on August 20. The focus is now steaks, seafood, housemade pastas and wood-fired sourdough flatbreads, with tableside martinis. Marathe is a James Beard Award semifinalist, so this is a serious kitchen aiming to be a neighborhood regular.

Nōmade

6030 SW 18th Street

The Westport, Connecticut, favorite made its Florida debut this spring with 250 seats — far more than the original. Executive chef Zoltán Kovács cooks plates from around the globe. Reports differ on the exact date, so I’ll simply say it opened around the end of May or early June.

MIN Omakase & Sake Bar

114 NE Second Street · minoomakase.com

This intimate downtown sushi counter serves a constantly changing tasting menu. It was slated to open May 1 — call ahead to confirm — and it’s the right call for a smaller celebration.

Limani Grille

Town Center at Boca Raton, 6000 Glades Road, main level

This is the opening Boca waited longest for, and it’s finally here. The New York-born Greek seafood restaurant was announced for spring 2024, then delayed by construction. Now open at Town Center at Boca Raton, it serves seafood grilled over charcoal in the Greek tradition, in an Aegean-inspired dining room with an expansive patio.

Parlor Doughnuts

9 Plaza Real South, Suite 108, Mizner Park

The layered-doughnut and coffee shop set a September 26 grand opening for its first Palm Beach County location, and the city now lists it as open. Vegan and gluten-free options are part of the lineup.

Two Boca Favorites Worth Your Reservation

These two aren’t new this year, but neither belongs off a list of where Boca is eating right now.

Motek Boca Raton opened in April 2025 at 5377 Town Center Road, Suite 300, on Restaurant Row. The Miami-born Mediterranean and Israeli-inspired restaurant has 220 seats, an open kitchen, and a 360-degree bar and wine room. Order the arayes burger, its signature dish.

Meat Market Boca Raton sits inside the Renaissance Boca Raton Hotel at 2000 NW 19th Street. This glamorous modern steakhouse, led by executive chef Sean Brasel, pairs prime steaks with fresh seafood, sushi and a raw bar. Sunday brunch comes with live music, and its private dining spaces suit a bigger celebration.

Now Open in Delray Beach

Delray’s newest tables are split among Pineapple Grove, the Atlantic Avenue core and Sundy Village, the mixed-use campus at Atlantic and Swinton.

Ethereal

324 NE Third Avenue, Unit 1 · Nightly, 5 to 11 p.m. · etherealdelray.com

If you want one new special-occasion room this season, start here. Designer Nick Alain and chef Estuardo Mira, the team behind Glimmer Cafe, opened it directly across the street from their café.

Boca magazine describes European- and Latin-inspired cooking, including mini lobster rolls, steak frites au poivre, zebra lobster ravioli and a white sturgeon caviar service. The room carries Alain’s taste for Victorian extravagance, and the desserts are built for the camera — including a cheesecake topped with a golden chandelier candle.

Preface Wine

279 NE Second Avenue, Pineapple Grove · preface.wine

Founder Jonathan Frutkin, known online as Wine with Jon, held his grand opening weekend October 22 to 25. It’s a wine shop by day and a 28-seat wine bar from 4 p.m., with tasting flights, charcuterie and a wine club. Frutkin’s pitch is wine without the intimidation.

Kapow Noodle Bar

32 SE Second Avenue · kapownoodlebar.com

Kapow has taken over the former Salt7 space, pairing a Pan-Asian menu with a nightlife program. The cocktails are curated by Angela Dugan, and the late-night happy hour includes half-off single liquor drinks, top shelf included.

Double Knot

22 W. Atlantic Avenue, Suite 150, Sundy Village · doubleknotdelray.com

The Philadelphia-born izakaya opened June 17 with sushi, sashimi, robatayaki, sake and craft cocktails. Choose à la carte or the chef’s tasting menu.

Barcelona Wine Bar

22 W. Atlantic Avenue, Suite 140, Sundy Village

The Spanish tapas and wine concept became the first full-service restaurant at Sundy Village on January 17. The wine list runs past 400 bottles.

Also Worth a Stop

The Boca Raton Observer’s 2026 roundup also flagged Tropical Smokehouse for Caribbean-inflected barbecue, Geronimo Tequila Bar & Southwest Grill for Santa Fe flavors, and Selini Coastal Mediterranean for sun-soaked seafood.

Felice

The family-owned group started on Manhattan’s Upper East Side in 2007 and already has West Palm Beach and Miami locations. Expect handmade pastas, regional Italian dishes and wines from the family’s own Tuscan vineyard, Fattoria Sardi. Look for it to open in late fall or winter 2026.

Fletchers

This may be the most ambitious project on the list. Former Olympian Michael Stember leads the kitchen at a 1970s Palm Beach-style supper club replacing Hijinks Sports Grill. Plans call for 120 seats, a chef-led omakase, Dover sole, sashimi and a late-night lounge, plus a 40-seat private club for paying members.

Maman in Delray Beach

Co-founder Elisa Marshall told the Palm Beach Post this will be the most unique of Maman’s three new local cafés, comparing it to a cute beach house. It will also have the most generous outdoor seating, which should make it a natural morning stop at Sundy Village.

Empire Steak House

The Sinanaj brothers — Jeff, Jack and Russ — learned the business as waiters at Peter Luger before building Empire. Their Delray location takes over the former CUT 432 space, with porterhouses, seafood and fine wines on Atlantic Avenue.

Drinking Pig BBQ

Chef Raheem Sealey’s Miami barbecue favorite has been promised for Sundy Village for some time. A July 4 goal came and went, as did a reported September target. I couldn’t confirm that it has opened, so check before you go.

Amal

This modern Lebanese restaurant, with locations in Coconut Grove and Toronto, has leased about 10,600 square feet at The Aletto, the new downtown project at 119 E. Palmetto Park Road. Plans call for a rooftop setting, and the same operator, INK Entertainment, is also bringing Sofia to the building. No opening date has been announced, and The Aletto is expected to be completed in 2027.

Bellini

The Cipriani family’s modern Italian restaurant will be the public dining room at Mr. C Residences, with carpaccio, handmade pastas and a landscaped terrace. It’s the longest wait on this list: it’s slated to open alongside the building’s 2029 completion.

What Could This Mean for Boca and Delray Real Estate?

Restaurants don’t set home values, but they do show where experienced operators are willing to bet. From my perspective in the Polo Club and the Boca–Delray corridor, three patterns stand out.

The Northeast is still moving in. Limani, Felice, Empire, Black Star and Nōmade all come from New York or Connecticut. Maman’s co-founder told the Palm Beach Post that migration from Miami and New York helped draw the brand to Boca and Delray. For buyers relocating from the Northeast, finding familiar names already here tends to make a new community feel like home a good deal sooner.

Dining corridors are spreading. West Boca gained Maman and Crema within weeks of each other, while Restaurant Row and Town Center are building a serious dinner destination west of I-95. Buyers who once assumed they had to live east for great dining now have more choices. That matters directly for communities like the Polo Club, which sits in exactly that western corridor.

Mixed-use is delivering. Sundy Village’s first phase was 88 percent leased at completion, and it brought 100,000 square feet of offices and 30,000 square feet of retail and dining to downtown Delray.

Here’s the other side. Busy dining districts can also bring traffic, parking pressure and late-night noise, and a restaurant boom alone doesn’t guarantee appreciation. Recent listing data showed about 8.4 months of condo and townhouse supply in Delray Beach on October 24, so buyers there still have options. Reasonable people can, and do, disagree on how much a new restaurant row is worth to a nearby home.

In plain English: walkability and dining are a real part of how buyers judge a neighborhood, but they should sit alongside price, condition and the specific street.
